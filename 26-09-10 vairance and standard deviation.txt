QUARTILES AND PERCENTILES

Quartiles
order your data and cutting it into 4 chunks
the center should be the median, and distribute your values
by instance
25th percentile is the first quarter of the values
50th percentile is the first two quartes
75th percentile

Percentiles - where something is relative to others
pth percentiles is a value such that at least p percent of the items
take on this value or less
If you are in the 80th percentile, there are 20% with a higher value

IQR - interquartile range
difference between quarters 1 and 3
ignores lower and upper ends.
takes a snapshot of the middle values

variance
measure how far observations are from the mean using
-squared differences

how to calc
set 4 4 6 6 7.6 8
1 find the mean 6
2 find how far (the difference) each observation is from the mean,
2 2 2 0 0 1.5
4 4 4 0 0 2.25 -> 2.375

Standard deviation SD
typical difference of observations from the mean
-> variance is the average squared diff.
standard dev is the suqare root of the vairance

small SD means values are tightly clustered

Histogram
similar to a bar chart - which compare different groupings
Histograms are grouping observations into ranges (bins) and show us how many values are in each range
- lets us see the shape of the data

each bin has a range of values that the values fit into
each bin shows the number of values that fall into it

Distribution shapes

Normal distribution - (bell curve)
symmetrical, where observations cluster around the center
and thin out toward the extreme

Center would be the median
heights in a population are approximately normal
right skewed - a long tail on the right

Bimodal
Two distinct peaks

Lognormal
A right-skewed distribution
the logarithm of the values is normally distributed


OUTLIERS
an observation that is unusually far from the rest
Commonly identified with the 1.5 IQR rule
Interquarterly range
lower fence is Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
Upper fence is Q1 + 1.5 * IQR
