Matching data types to chart types

continuous data describes a variable that can take any value within a range.
can have decimal places. like a float
temperature, height, elapsed time


discrete data takes distinct values
number of whole things, or people
Only whole numbers, like an integer

real world continuous measurments are often rounded

Normal distributions
68% are within 1 standard deviation
95% are within 2 sd
99.7% within 3 sd

you can assume a normal distribution for large datasets, but not always

Other continuous distributions
Uniform distributions: everything is equally likely
lognormal distributions: sharp left peak and long right tail

exponential distribution
starts at max, and slides smoothly down towards zero, but never meets zero

Real-life examples:
Adult heights
Personal incomes, often right skewed
Delivery times, often right skewed
Exam scores may be bimodal, if two groups experience different levels of preparation


Histogram
shows frequency of numeric observations within intervals called bins
good for showing skewedness,
changing bin width can change the apparent shape
wide bins can hide detail
bins must be same width

Box Plot
How do groups compare
Summarizes median, quartiles, and potential outliers
useful for comparing distributions across categories

top of box line is Q3, center line is median,
bottom of box line is Q1

Line chart
Generally, how does something change over time
Great for seeing trends
connects observations in a meaningful sequence, usually time
can compare several values

Dont use for thing that dont have a sequence

scatter plots
Are two numeric variables related?
Is there a correlation?
Show paired obserbations for two numeric variables

helps reveal relationship, clusters and unusual observations


Picking colors for charts
generally pick company colors

Sequential palettes
Use a progression of colors ranging in intensity, light to dark,
when values have natural order

Diverging palettes
show two contrasting colour directions around a meaningful midpoint

qualitative palettes
use distinct colors to distinguish categories 

The job of colour
Can encode information
highlight a finding, establish hierarchy,
Avoid assigning color to every mark when the color has no purpose
(avoid color barf)
Use neutral base palette, reserve stronger colour for the most important info
Keep colour meanings consistent across related charts. If green means good, dont use it for anything else
Dont use red and green as the only distinct between two important values - Red Green color blindness is a thing

Accessability 
Dont over-rely on color. Make sure there are more ways to show the info

Check in grayscale to see if the graph is readable
